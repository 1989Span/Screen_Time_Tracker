Gauge Privacy Policy

Effective September 24, 2026

In short: Gauge shows you how much time you spend in the apps on your phone. Everything it reads and stores stays on your phone, unless you choose to share your daily totals with a group. Gauge has no account, no server, no ads and no analytics, and it never sends your data anywhere by itself.

What Gauge reads

  • App usage. With your permission, Gauge reads Android's record of which apps were in the foreground and for how long. Android keeps this behind a setting called Usage access (on some phones, Usage data access), which only you can turn on in Android's settings. Gauge cannot turn it on for you.
  • Your list of apps. Gauge reads the names of the apps on your phone that have a home-screen icon, so you can choose which ones to track.

Gauge does not read your messages, contacts, photos, location, browsing history, or anything you type or view inside other apps. It only sees which app was open and for how long.

What Gauge stores, and where

Gauge saves daily totals of time spent per app in a database on your phone, so it can show your week, month and year. It also saves your settings on your phone: which apps you track and any daily limits you set. A background task updates these totals about once a day, so your history keeps building even when you don't open the app.

Usage history is kept for about 400 days, and older days are deleted automatically.

If you add Gauge's home-screen widget, it reads the same data on your phone to show a total, and it saves which time range each widget shows. That also stays on your phone.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Gauge reminds you each time your screen time today passes another hour. These reminders are created on your phone from the same data. Nothing is sent to a server to produce them. You can turn them off with the Hourly nudges switch in Gauge's Settings, or in Android's notification settings.

What Gauge shares

Nothing by itself. Gauge never sends your data to the developer, and it contains no advertising, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking software from other companies. Your data is not sold or used for advertising.

Groups: what you share, and only when you choose

Groups let friends compare screen time. There is no server behind them. When you tap Share my day or Invite, Gauge makes a link and opens your phone's share sheet. You pick who it goes to and which app sends it, such as your text messages. The link contains:

  • the group's name, and the name you chose to show in groups;
  • your total screen time for each of the last 14 days at most, counting every app except any the group agreed to leave out. It never lists which apps you used;
  • the apps you have voted to leave out of the group's totals, if any;
  • the names and daily totals of the other members, as last shared with you, so one message keeps the whole group up to date.

Gauge saves what other members share with you on your phone, and theirs saves what you share with them. Group links point to a page on this site, but the numbers sit after the # in the link, a part browsers never send to a website, so this site never receives them.

Once you send a link, the people you sent it to have it. Leaving a group stops future sharing, but it can't take back what was already sent.

Android lists Gauge as having internet access, because the framework the app is built with includes it by default. Gauge does not use it to send or receive your data.

Your choices

  • Stop access at any time by turning off Usage access for Gauge in Android settings. Gauge will stop reading new usage.
  • Stop sharing with a group by leaving it in Gauge, or simply by not tapping Share. Nothing is ever shared without you tapping it.
  • Delete your data by uninstalling Gauge, or through Android Settings > Apps > Gauge > Storage > Clear data. That deletes everything on your phone. The developer holds no copy. Anything you already sent to a group stays with the people you sent it to.

Children

Gauge is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. Because Gauge collects nothing off the device, no child's data ever reaches the developer.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If Gauge ever starts sending data off your phone, the policy will say so clearly before the app update that does it.
