Gauge Privacy Policy

Effective October 6, 2026

In short: Gauge shows you how much time you spend in the apps on your phone. Everything it reads and stores stays on your phone, with one exception: if you create or join a group, Gauge shares your display name and your total screen time for each day with the other members, automatically, after you agree to it. Gauge has no ads, no analytics and no sign-up.

What Gauge reads

  • App usage. With your permission, Gauge reads Android's record of which apps were in the foreground and for how long. Android keeps this behind a setting called Usage access (on some phones, Usage data access), which only you can turn on in Android's settings. Gauge cannot turn it on for you.
  • Unlocks. Through the same Usage access, Gauge reads when your phone was unlocked, so it can show how many times you unlock it each day. It stores only a count per day, on your phone, and never shares it, not even with a group.
  • Your list of apps. Gauge reads the names of the apps on your phone that have a home-screen icon, so you can choose which ones to track.

Gauge does not read your messages, contacts, photos, location, browsing history, or anything you type or view inside other apps. It only sees which app was open and for how long.

What Gauge stores, and where

Gauge saves daily totals of time spent per app in a database on your phone, so it can show your week, month and year. It also saves your settings on your phone: which apps you track and any daily limits you set. A background task updates these totals about once a day, so your history keeps building even when you don't open the app.

Usage history is kept for about 400 days, and older days are deleted automatically.

If you add Gauge's home-screen widget, it reads the same data on your phone to show a total, and it saves which time range each widget shows. That also stays on your phone.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Gauge reminds you each time your screen time today passes another hour. These reminders are created on your phone from the same data. Nothing is sent to a server to produce them. You can turn them off with the Hourly nudges switch in Gauge's Settings, or in Android's notification settings.

If you're in a group, Gauge also notifies you when another member asks to stop tracking an app in the group, and, if you asked, how each member votes. These come from the group server, as described below. You can turn them off in Android's notification settings, under Group requests.

What Gauge shares

Outside of Groups, nothing. Gauge never sends your usage data to the developer, and it contains no advertising, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking software from other companies. Your data is not sold or used for advertising.

Groups: what is shared, and only if you join

Groups let friends compare screen time. They are the only part of Gauge that sends anything off your phone, and only once you create or join a group. If you never use Groups, nothing leaves your phone.

Before you create or join a group, Gauge shows you what will be shared and asks you to agree. From then on it keeps the group up to date automatically, about once an hour, so the scores are accurate without anyone having to remember to share. To do that, the group server sends your phone a silent push at the top of each hour. It shows nothing and contains nothing about you; it only tells Gauge to sync. For each group you're in, it sends:

  • the name you chose to show in groups, and the date you joined;
  • your total screen time for each day you've been in the group, counting every app except any the group agreed to leave out. It never sends which apps you used, or when;
  • any apps you vote to leave out of the group's totals, by their name and Android package name (such as com.example.app), and whether you were the one who asked;
  • a push token: a random address that lets the group server send notifications to this copy of Gauge. It identifies the app on your phone, not you, and is never shown to other members.

If you create a group, its name is stored too.

Where it goes, and who can see it

Group data is stored by Supabase, the hosting provider Gauge uses for groups, on servers in the United States, and it travels over encrypted connections. Supabase handles it only to run the service. Only members of the same group can see it, and the database itself enforces that. The developer can reach it only as the administrator of that database, and doesn't use it for anything else. Like most web services, Supabase keeps short-lived technical logs of connections, such as IP addresses, for security and troubleshooting.

There is no sign-up and no email address. The first time you create or join a group, the server gives your phone a random, anonymous ID, which Gauge keeps so the server knows which totals are yours.

Group notifications

When a member asks to stop tracking an app, or votes on that request, the group server sends a notification to the other members' phones (or to the person who asked). It goes through Expo's push notification service and Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, which deliver it to your phone. The notification contains the member's display name, the app's name and the group's name. Expo and Google handle it only to deliver it.

Invite links contain the group's name, the inviter's name and a random invite code, and nothing else. They point to a page on this site, but that information sits after the # in the link, a part browsers never send to a website, so this site never receives it.

How long it's kept

Daily totals older than 400 days are deleted automatically. If your phone doesn't sync with a group for 400 days, for example because you uninstalled Gauge, you're removed from the group and your totals are deleted. A group is deleted when its last member leaves. Your push token is deleted when you leave your last group.

Your choices

  • Stop access at any time by turning off Usage access for Gauge in Android settings. Gauge will stop reading new usage.
  • Leave a group in Gauge, under the group's settings. Your name, votes and daily totals are deleted from that group on the server straight away, and nothing more is sent to it.
  • Delete your group data in Gauge, under any group's settings, with Delete my group data. That deletes all your group memberships, daily totals and votes, your push token and your anonymous ID from the server.
  • Already uninstalled Gauge? Email c2lomon@gmail.com with the group's name and the name you used in it, and your group data will be deleted. It is also deleted automatically after 400 days without a sync.
  • Delete everything on your phone by uninstalling Gauge, or through Android Settings > Apps > Gauge > Storage > Clear data. If you're in a group, use Delete my group data first, because clearing the phone doesn't reach the server.

Children

Gauge is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has joined a group, email the address below and their group data will be deleted.
